# Allocator and archive restore witness (WIP)

Base: `71b3384193a80eb8d0a122eb439e51973508356a`. This adds a synthetic PostgreSQL witness only; it does not change product code, migrations, SMS, or orthodontic workflows.

The archive witness creates new, uniquely named local source and target databases, retains their OID/owner records and all generated evidence, and creates an actual full archive using the application writer. It checks the staged restore, patient/document/invoice/retired-alias/policy/audit snapshots, linked document metadata and bytes, and allocator state. It then attempts the same complete archive again with the default nonempty-target policy and checks that the target and document remain unchanged. The existing backup writer internally removes its own short-lived snapshot staging directory; the witness retains the final archive, restored files, identity record, and databases.

An independent SQL witness exercises the historical #490 matrix on a freshly migrated target: three current high-water positions crossed with both source and target `is_called` states, two applications per case, and the next allocation. Its expected values are explicitly enumerated, rather than derived from the product formula. Another case puts a retired alias and numbering policy above the sequence, applies the SQL twice, and inserts the allocated synthetic patient. The restored archive target retains its sequence and rows instead of being repurposed for the matrix. Replaying the full archive is not equivalent to replaying its allocator statement: staged restore rejects a populated target by default; allowing a nonempty target does not establish that re-inserting all rows is safe or idempotent. This witness calls the application restore function directly, not the HTTP endpoint; it compares six named tables and linked document bytes, not every table.

The test is included only in the dedicated standalone witness configuration. Its global setup validates the dedicated GitHub Actions job and exact disposable PostgreSQL 18 service before opening its first connection. The fixture repeats that validation, creates only new uniquely named databases, validates their identities, and issues no DROP or evidence deletion. `TEST_DATABASE_URL` is used only to derive the validated maintenance connection and is not reset by this test. In CI the temporary databases and files are available only while that runner and service remain alive; they are not uploaded or guaranteed to persist after teardown. The environment checks are safeguards against accidental invocation, not cryptographic proof of service ownership outside the declared CI job.

On the earlier published head `e69e2f088c16892c71f6208f553b83fdda79b9ed`, [CI 44083373433](https://github.com/Aqlanf10/aqlan-center-mini/actions/runs/44083373433) passed this original archive and matrix witness and the separate historical/fresh standalone HTTP witness. The full CI failed in both HTTP shards. Its successful allocator witness did not show whether a restored policy or retired alias above the sequence drives the next number.

This later local WIP adds two independent application-written full archives, one with a higher policy and an uncalled sequence, and one with a higher retired identifier and a called sequence. Each compares the six source/target tables, linked document bytes and sequence, then allocates and inserts a new synthetic patient on the restored target and verifies the source, archive and documents remain unchanged. These new scenarios have not yet run on PostgreSQL or CI; the earlier CI result does not cover them. They do not test the HTTP endpoint or 0090. Vitest failures may print synthetic fixture values, and CI databases and files are ephemeral. No deploy or merge is authorized by this report.
