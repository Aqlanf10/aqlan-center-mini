# Service-specific material consumption release proof

The owner clarified that material consumption runs on the amount left after the lab and other enabled direct costs, before applying the doctor's percentage. A fixed 20% of 10,000 when a 2,000 lab cost was already taken is not the accepted rule; 20% of 8,000 is 1,600 and the doctor's 40% is 2,560. Existing actual-material exemption, manual lab-deduction opt-out, currency buckets, payment-time source and payroll posting rules remain separate.

The service policy is keyed by the catalog service ID, not by labels or specialties. A positive rate takes precedence over its category, a recorded zero explicitly exempts the service, and a new null revision reverts to the category prospectively. Previous revisions stay present, a change requires an audited reason and matching revision, and the canonical route uses the finance-settings capability. Historical collection timestamps continue to select the rate. The existing material toggle still controls whether the rate affects the earned amount. No payroll or ledger records are migrated.

**Release blocker:** schema 0143 is a rehearsal proposal, not an active migration. Its absence keeps the separate UI and writes inactive and retains category behavior. Do not release the feature or silently remove the table once any service policy has been recorded. Before activation, reserve the final migration ID across parallel proposals, add the table to schema ownership, backup/restore and clinic-reset contracts, and prove old-client compatibility, effective-timestamp integrity and fallback/reconciliation after restore. Validate the reviewer for Settings, the mixed invoice, finalized and paid payroll, and the summary versus detail views, on an isolated synthetic database and exact hosted commit.

A native PG18.6 fixture demonstrates 8,000 at 20% plus 5,000 at 10% on one invoice in a single category with a conflicting category default, at a 40% doctor rate: 4,360 in both report and draft payroll. Changes after the collection do not change that historical earning or the approved payroll row. Another fixture proves the owner's 10,000/2,000 lab example. The mixed fixture deliberately has no lab cost; it does not prove exact attribution of a lab cost to one of several same-doctor services. Legacy lab costs with only an invoice/doctor reference are still allocated proportionally under the existing rules and should not be represented as line-specific without stored lineage.
